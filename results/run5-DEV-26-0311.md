## DEV-26-0311: B261284 Atorvastatin 20 mg, compression on T-1590

**Proposed classification: Major.** Raise it to Critical if the load cell turns out to have been reading low, or if the earlier batches are found to be affected.

### Root cause (probable, not yet confirmed)
- **The force sensor (load cell) on T-1590 was past its calibration due date.** It was due on 2026-09-03 (last calibrated 2026-03-07 on a 180-day interval) and was never taken out of service. Because the sensor wasn't in a verified state, we can't tell whether the force really rose or the reading drifted.
- **A mechanical cause is also possible.** The operator wrote that the machine was "running rough since the changeover". This needs checking under maintenance ticket MNT-3312.
- **Confirming the cause needs three things:**
  - an as-found calibration of the sensor before anyone adjusts it;
  - a mechanical inspection;
  - a review of the hourly force records in the batch record, alongside tablet weight, hardness and thickness data.

**Contributing factors:**
- The calibration system has no lockout, so an overdue instrument can still be used.
- Warnings were not escalated. On 09-09 there was a drift to 18.4 kN during B261277 (deviation DEV-26-0298). On 09-14 the operator flagged the out-of-date calibration sticker. Both times the response was "continue and monitor" instead of stopping and telling QA.
- After the alarm at 09:52, the force stayed above the limit for about 18 minutes before anyone acted. The machine wasn't stopped until 11:18.
- At 10:15 the operator changed the setpoint to 16.5 kN by hand, with no recorded approval. That was after the excursion had already ended, so it fixed nothing and changed the process for the rest of the run.

**Ruled out:**
- **Training:** OP-114 is trained on the current SOP versions.
- **Environment:** conditions were within limits on 09-09 and 09-14.
- **Tooling gauge:** calibration is in date.
- **Raw material as the only cause:** the same material lots were used in B261270, which had no problems. One thing to check: the API assay is recorded as 90.8%, so confirm the basis and that the dispensing potency adjustment was right.

### Impact
- **B261284:**
  - Reject the tablets set aside from the excursion window.
  - Keep the rest on hold. The sensor was out of calibration for the whole run, so every force reading is unverified, and tablets made from 10:15 to 11:18 were produced at the unapproved setpoint.
  - Run layered testing on each portion of the run: hardness, friability, disintegration, full dissolution profile (a key attribute for atorvastatin), assay and content uniformity.
- **B261277 and B261270 are also affected.** This goes beyond the reported batch. Both were compressed on T-1590 after the 09-03 due date, and B261277 also had the 09-09 drift. Confirm now whether they've been released or distributed. Put them on hold if they're still in company control, and assess whether they need a market or recall review.

### CAPA
- **Immediate:**
  - Take T-1590 out of service.
  - Do the as-found calibration.
  - Put the two earlier batches on hold.
  - Finish the maintenance investigation.
  - Retrieve DEV-26-0298 and link it as a recurrence.
- **Corrective:**
  - Check every GMP instrument for overdue calibration.
  - Retrain supervisors and maintenance to stop production and notify QA when an instrument is out of calibration or a limit is exceeded.
  - Reinforce with compression operators how to respond to alarms and how to get approval for setpoint changes.
- **Preventive:**
  - Add a system block that stops equipment with overdue calibration from being used.
  - Make a sustained high-force alarm reject tablets or stop the machine automatically.
  - Revise SOP-PRD-014 so every excursion requires a deviation and QA notice, and manual setpoint changes are controlled.
- **Effectiveness check:** three months with no instrument used past its due date and no repeat of this kind of event.

### Problems found in the records
- **Event order:** the shift log puts the setpoint change before the alarm, but the equipment timestamps show it came 23 minutes after. The shift log looks like it was written after the fact, so get statements from the operator and supervisor.
- **Missing deviation:** DEV-26-0298 is referenced for B261277 but isn't in the deviation file I was given.
- **Unrelated record:** the M Block environmental record for 09-18 shows the humidity limit as "NLT 6", which looks like a typo.

The investigation should stay **open** until the as-found calibration result is in.
