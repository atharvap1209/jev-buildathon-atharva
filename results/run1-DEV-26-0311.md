## DEV-26-0311: B261284, Atorvastatin 20 mg, compression on T-1590

**Proposed classification: Major.** Escalate to Critical if an affected batch has already been released and then fails testing.

**Root cause (probable): the calibration system let an overdue instrument stay in use.** The compression force load cell on T-1590 was due for calibration on 2026-09-03 (certificate CAL-26-0771, status OVERDUE). Nothing stopped it being used, and it ran for three batches after that date. Because it wasn't calibrated, none of its force readings can be trusted: not the 19.6 kN alarm, and not the readings that looked normal.

**Contributing factors:**
- **Warnings weren't escalated.**
  - On 09-09, B261277 drifted to 18.4 kN for 6 minutes (DEV-26-0298), and the supervisor said to "monitor".
  - On 09-14 the operator reported the out-of-date calibration sticker, and maintenance still said to carry on (ticket MNT-3312).
- **The alarm response was slow.** The force was above the limit for about 18 minutes before anyone acted. The machine was stopped at 11:18, about 86 minutes after the 09:52 alarm.
- **Unauthorised setpoint change.** At 10:15 the operator lowered the setpoint to 16.5 kN by hand, with no record of approval. This could hide the underlying fault.
- **A mechanical cause isn't ruled out.** The log says the machine was "running rough since the changeover", so worn tooling or a mechanical fault is still possible.

**Ruled out or unlikely:** the room conditions on 09-14 were all within limits, and OP-114's training on the current SOPs is up to date. Materials are unlikely, because B261270 used the same lots with no excursion. However, the API lot's recorded assay of 90.8% looks low and should be checked against its certificate of analysis.

**Confirming the cause:** check the load cell's accuracy before it is adjusted (the as-found result), and get the MNT-3312 findings. If the load cell turns out to have been accurate, the high force was real and the investigation should shift to the mechanical cause.

**Impact assessment:**
- **B261284:** keep the whole batch on hold, not just the 18-minute window. I propose rejecting the segregated tablets. Test samples from before, during and after the excursion, and after the setpoint change, for hardness, thickness, friability, disintegration, dissolution profile, assay, content uniformity and weight variation.
- **B261277 and B261270:** both were also compressed on the overdue load cell; B261270 started on 09-05, two days after the due date. Hold them if not yet released, test retained samples, and link DEV-26-0298 to this deviation. If either has been distributed, assess the market impact.
- **Metformin batches:** they didn't use T-1590 and aren't affected. DEV-26-0412 is a separate event.

**CAPA:**
- **Immediate corrections:**
  - Quarantine T-1590.
  - Record the as-found accuracy, then recalibrate.
  - Close MNT-3312 with a documented mechanical inspection.
  - Put the two earlier batches on hold.
- **Corrective actions:**
  - Check every instrument on site for overdue calibration.
  - Re-open DEV-26-0298.
  - Retrain on alarm response, escalation, and the ban on unauthorised setpoint changes.
- **Preventive actions:**
  - Block use of any instrument past its calibration due date, with alerts at 30, 14 and 7 days before.
  - Revise SOP-PRD-014 to say what to do on a compression force alarm or drift.
  - Make any overdue-calibration or drift report go straight to QA.
  - Review compression force alarms monthly.
- **Effectiveness check:** over 6 months, no instrument used past its due date, and every alarm escalated to QA on time.
