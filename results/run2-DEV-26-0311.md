**Classification: Major (proposed).** Compression force is a critical process parameter, and it went above the validated limit while the instrument measuring it was past its calibration due date. It also happened before, and the earlier warning wasn't acted on.

**What the records show:**
- **Overdue calibration:** the compression force load cell on T-1590 was due for calibration on 2026-09-03. It was still overdue on 09-14, and the equipment stayed in use.
- **Earlier warning:** on 09-09, batch B261277 went to 18.4 kN for 6 minutes. The shift log says the reading was "jumpy" and the supervisor said to "monitor".
- **On 09-14:**
  - The operator (OP-114) noticed the out-of-date calibration sticker and flagged it.
  - Maintenance said to "continue and monitor" (ticket MNT-3312). The machine had been "running rough since the changeover".
  - The alarm went off at 09:52: 19.6 kN for 18 minutes.
  - At 10:15 the operator lowered the setpoint to 16.5 kN by hand, with no recorded authorisation.
  - The machine wasn't stopped until 11:18, 1 h 26 min after the alarm.
- **Ruled out:** operator training is current, and room conditions were within limits. The raw materials were the same lots used in the earlier batches.

**Root cause (medium confidence):** the calibration system didn't stop an overdue instrument being used, and there was an unresolved mechanical or setup problem after the changeover. Contributing causes were the early warnings not being escalated, maintenance approving continued running without QA, the late stop, and the unauthorised setpoint change. Until the load cell's as-found calibration result is in, we can't tell whether the tablets were really over-compressed or the instrument was reading high.

**Impact:**
- **B261284:** stays on hold. Test the segregated tablets separately from the rest of the batch. Dissolution matters most, because atorvastatin dissolves poorly and over-compression can slow it.
- **B261277 and B261270:** both were also compressed on T-1590 after the calibration due date. Check whether they've been released and review their data.
- **Further back:** if the as-found calibration fails, every batch compressed on T-1590 since the last good calibration (2026-03-07) comes into scope.
- **Not affected:** the Metformin batches didn't use T-1590.

**CAPA:**
- **Immediate:** take T-1590 out of service, get the as-found calibration before anything is adjusted, finish MNT-3312, and check the whole site for other overdue instruments still in use.
- **Corrective:**
  - Block equipment with overdue calibration from being used for production.
  - Revise SOP-PRD-014 so any reading over the limit means stop the machine and raise a deviation, and manual setpoint changes need authorisation.
  - Maintenance can't approve continued running when an instrument is out of calibration; that decision goes to QA.
  - Retrain the operators, supervisors and maintenance staff involved.
- **Preventive:** calibration due-date alerts, trending of compression force with early-warning limits, a review of the load cell's calibration interval, and the same controls applied to other equipment.
- **Effectiveness check:** due 2027-05-31.
