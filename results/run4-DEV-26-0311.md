## DEV-26-0311 – B261284 Atorvastatin 20 mg, compression on tablet press T-1590

**Proposed classification: Major.** Raise it to Critical if the load cell turns out to be reading significantly wrong, or if an affected batch has already been released.

**What happened:**
- **Sep 3:** the compression force load cell on T-1590 fell due for calibration and wasn't done. It was 11 days overdue on the day of the event.
- **Sep 5 and Sep 9:** B261270 and B261277 were both compressed on the press after that due date. On Sep 9 the force drifted to 18.4 kN (limit 18.0) for 6 minutes during B261277. The supervisor said to monitor it; that batch has its own deviation, DEV-26-0298.
- **Sep 14, before the alarm:** the operator reported the press "running rough since the changeover" and flagged the out-of-date calibration sticker. Maintenance said to carry on and monitor (ticket MNT-3312).
- **09:52:** alarm at 19.6 kN, lasting 18 minutes.
- **10:15:** the operator lowered the force setpoint by hand, with no recorded authorisation.
- **11:18:** machine stopped, 1 h 26 min after the alarm.

**Where the records conflict:**
- The deviation says the machine was stopped as the immediate action, but it actually kept running for another 86 minutes.
- The shift log lists the setpoint change before the alarm. The equipment log shows it came after.
- The deviation doesn't mention the overdue calibration, even though it was flagged before the excursion.

**Root cause (medium confidence):** calibration and equipment control failed. Nothing stopped an overdue instrument being used, and maintenance told the operator to keep going after the problem was raised. On top of that, a mechanical fault was left unfixed: it showed as drift on Sep 9 and as rough running after the Sep 14 changeover. The exact physical cause of the force rise isn't confirmed. That needs the as-found load cell reading and the MNT-3312 inspection results.

**Ruled out:**
- **Operator training:** OP-114 is trained on the current SOP versions.
- **Environment:** temperature, humidity and pressure were all within limits.
- **Materials:** unlikely, because the same material lots went into B261270, which had no problems. The API lot L26-0884 assay of 90.8% looks low, though, and should be checked against its specification.

**Impact:**
- **B261284:** keep on hold. Propose rejecting the segregated tablets. Release the rest only after extra testing (hardness, friability, disintegration, dissolution, assay/content uniformity) and after the load cell reading is known. With the load cell unverified, the "within range" readings outside the alarm window can't be trusted either.
- **B261277:** high risk. It had a recorded excursion while the load cell was overdue.
- **B261270:** medium risk. Its readings are also unverified.
- For both, check release status and do a market-impact assessment if either has shipped. Any other product run on T-1590 since Sep 3 is in scope too.
- **Patient risk:** mainly slower dissolution and lower effectiveness.
- **Compliance:** using an out-of-calibration instrument in production is a GMP gap.

**CAPA:**
- **Corrections:** tag T-1590 out of service and record the as-found calibration before adjusting anything; finish MNT-3312; do the extra testing; put B261270/B261277 on hold if still on site and link DEV-26-0298.
- **Corrective actions:** review calibration status for every instrument on site; retrain supervisors and maintenance that an overdue calibration or a limit breach means stop and tell QA, not "continue and monitor"; revise SOP-PRD-014 so setpoint changes after an alarm need approval and the machine stops straight away.
- **Preventive actions:** block equipment with overdue calibration from starting production; send calibration reminders 30 and 7 days ahead; require an investigation of any force drift before the press is used again. Each has an effectiveness check.

**Needed before closure:**
- The DEV-26-0298 record, which isn't in the data
- Release status of B261270 and B261277
- The batch record's hourly force entries
- An interview with OP-114 and the maintenance and supervisory staff about the "continue and monitor" instruction
